# Text type: website copy

For swiyu.admin.ch – pages, overlays, card sets, FAQs, tables, link text, meta descriptions. Audience rules are in `SKILL.md` §3; this file governs form.

**Pages stay light and scannable; depth goes into an overlay.**
- Every block stays short enough to scan. A subject that needs sustained explanation moves into an overlay with its fixed structure.
- This is about the length of each block, not the amount on the page. A page still covers its subjects in full sections. Thin is not the same as light.
- Length is not the problem to solve. A block over budget, or a section wanting a third idea, is the signal to move material into an overlay. Not to write longer, add a card, or cut substance to fit.

**Vocabulary.**
- A page is a sequence of sections. A section is a label, a heading, and the blocks under them. A block is one heading with its text, the smallest piece that stands on its own. A card is a block; a step is a block.
- In a delivered draft the elements are named as in the table below – *section label*, *h1*, *h2*, *h3*, *h4*, *lead*, *body*, *button label* – and nothing else.

Sections 1, 2, 8 and 9 apply to everything; 3 to 7 each add rules for one kind of text.

## 1 Length budgets

Characters, including spaces, measured against the finalised pages. Budget is the target, cap the hard limit. A real constraint given for a specific element wins. The body cap is per paragraph; line-break segments count separately. French runs 15 to 20 per cent longer than English and decides whether a constrained label fits (WRD-09).

| Element | Typical | Budget | Cap |
|---|---|---|---|
| section label above the h1 | 16 | 24 | 30 |
| h1 | 52 | 67 | 90 |
| section label above an h2 | 15 | 23 | 32 |
| h2 | 35 | 65 | 86 |
| h3 | 24 | 41 | 70 |
| h4 | 30 | 40 | 60 |
| lead | not yet measured | body budget until it is | 254 |
| body | 115 | 254 | 254 per paragraph |
| button label | 13 | 19 | 25 |

## 2 Rules for every piece of website copy

**STR-01 One idea and one argumentative role per block, one subject per section.**
- Two blocks making the same argument means one is redundant; a block making two needs to split.
- A section covers one subject: security and privacy, the ecosystem, getting started. Decide the subjects first; they set the section list. Two unrelated ideas in one section is the commonest structural fault.

**STR-02 A heading states a finding, not a topic.** *Sicherheit* names a subject; *Jede Änderung wird bei der Prüfung sichtbar* answers a question. Section labels are the exception (STR-04).

### Links and buttons

**BTN-01 Four words maximum.**

**BTN-02 Where the label can be written, it names its destination.** *Mehr erfahren* alone gives reader and search engine nothing.

**BTN-03 Where the label is fixed, the surrounding text carries the destination.** Most cards cannot take a custom label; that is a constraint to write around, not an error to report. Heading and text above the button make the destination unambiguous; the generic label only confirms it (STR-08).

**BTN-04 One primary action per card.** Larger blocks are not bound by this; the hero carries a primary and a secondary.

## 3 Pages and sections

**STR-03 The h1 is the page title and appears once,** in the opening section, stating the outcome rather than the mechanism. Everything below is h2 or lower, however prominent it looks.

**STR-04 A section is a label above a heading, doing different jobs.** The label names the topic in a couple of words: *Vorteile*, *Anwendungsbeispiele*, *Hilfe*. The heading makes the claim: *Das bietet swiyu für Ihre Organisation*. Neither works alone.
- The label is not a shortened heading. No sentence, no verb, no echo of the words below it.

**STR-05 Reuse the label where the job is the same.** *Loslegen*, *Anwendungsbeispiele*, *Vorteile*, *Hilfe*, *Mitwirken* recur across pages with the same meaning. Take the existing label; keep the heading page-specific.

**STR-06 A content section carries a set of blocks, not one.** On the current pages content sections carry two to five, most often three or four; sections with none are openings and calls to action. A heading with a single paragraph has skipped the work of finding what the subject breaks into.

## 4 Overlays

An overlay is the popup that opens over a page, where a subject gets the depth the page cannot carry. Five parts, in order:

1. **Header and teaser** – what this is, in two sentences; must not pre-empt part 2.
2. **Why does this matter?** – the problem, its context, its stakes.
3. **What do you gain?** – bulleted benefits, each a standalone benefit sentence, never a feature.
4. **How does it work technically?** – the mechanism, in sub-sections.
5. **Where are the limits?** – what the technology does not solve.

**STR-07 The five parts are why depth belongs here.** An overlay without them is a long page in a smaller window.
- Parts 2 and 3 do not restate each other: the problem, then the reader's gain.
- Part 5 is not optional. Its absence is reported.
- Not every overlay is an explainer. A walkthrough is a numbered sequence (§6), a comparison a table under a short lead. Neither takes the five parts; a missing limits part in either is not a fault.

## 5 Card sets

A card is a heading with a short paragraph, leading somewhere: clickable as a whole or through its own button. It is read out of order, beside unrelated neighbours, often alone on a narrow screen.

**STR-08 A card stands alone and matches its neighbours.**
- It names its own destination. Title and text together make clear where it leads: *Webinar* could be a description, a recording or a registration; *Für ein Webinar anmelden* could not. Nothing outside the card supplies this.
- It reads independently. Sets reflow between one and four columns, so no title depends on its neighbours, its position, or the heading above the set.
- Cards in a set are parallel in construction, length and level of abstraction.

**STR-09 Repeated or empty cards are unfinished, not deliberate.** The same card on two pages, or an empty one, was carried over or left open in the design. Report the gap rather than polishing it into place.

## 6 Numbered sequences

**STR-10 Steps are parallel:** one heading plus one sentence each, the same grammatical form throughout, and the number adds nothing the heading does not.

## 7 FAQs

**STR-11 The question is phrased as the reader would ask it,** not as the organisation would title it.

**STR-12 The answer opens with the answer.** Context follows.

## 8 Search

Standard on-page practice applies and is not restated. Where a ranking tactic conflicts with the brand rules, the brand rules win: no keyword stuffing, no superlatives, the glossary term over the higher-volume synonym (WRD-04).

**SEO-01 One page, one primary term,** decided before writing, in the glossary's exact form. It appears in the h1 and within the first 100 words. A page competing with itself ranks for neither.

**SEO-02 A title tag of 55 to 60 characters and a meta description of 140 to 160.** The title puts the primary term first and differs from the h1; engines rewrite descriptions often and titles rarely. The description is benefit-led, active, contains the primary term and reads as a sentence.
- Keyword research per language, never by translating a German set.

## 9 Accessibility

swiyu is bound by eCH-0059 v3.0, which adopts WCAG 2.1 AA. Follow it; this file does not restate it.
- What WCAG 2.2 added is interaction and interface, which copy can neither satisfy nor violate.
- Link purpose, descriptive headings and page titles are covered above (BTN-02, BTN-03, STR-02, SEO-02). Truncation follows the UX copy file (UXC-10).
- **Where this file and the standards differ, this file decides.** Its rules are the stricter form.

**ACC-01 Abbreviations expanded at first use on every page.** Readers arrive mid-site.
