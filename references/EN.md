# English

Open this file when writing or correcting English. No federal writing rules cover English, so this file is the whole authority.
- Where it is silent, follow ordinary British editorial practice and add the decision here rather than deciding twice.
- Dashes, exclamation marks, abbreviations and the lowercase *swiyu* are settled in `SKILL.md` (MEC-06, MEC-08, MEC-09, NAM-01) and not repeated here.

---

## 1 Variety and spelling

**ENG-01 British English:** *-ise* endings and *-our* spellings. *organise*, *recognise*, *behaviour*, *favour*.
- Oxford *-ize* is also British but reads as American to most people; swiyu does not use it.

**ENG-02 British vocabulary:** *mobile* not *cell phone*, *post* not *mail*, *organisation* not *organization*.

**ENG-03 Keep Swiss terms in Swiss form:** *Confederation*, *canton*, *commune*, *Federal Chancellery*. Do not translate an institution's name into a foreign equivalent that suggests a different body.

---

## 2 Punctuation

**ENG-04 Quotation marks follow MEC-05:** single marks, double nested, punctuation by sense. Worked examples, because this is the rule most often carried over from American habit:
- ✅ *She said she felt 'free from care and anxiety'.*
- ✅ *'Today,' she said, 'the register goes live.'*
- 🚫 *She said she felt 'free from care and anxiety.'*

**ENG-05 No serial comma** before the final *and* in a list, unless leaving it out creates a real ambiguity.

---

## 3 Contractions

**ENG-06 Use common contractions** to keep sentences from stiffening: *what's, you'll, you're, you've, they're, doesn't, didn't, isn't, aren't, can't*.

**ENG-07 Not colloquial ones:** no *ain't*, no *y'all*, no *gonna*.

**ENG-08 Do not contract a noun with *is*, *has*, *does* or *was*.** *The register's open* reads as a possessive on first pass.

**ENG-09 SND-01 still applies.** *We'll* contracts a pronoun swiyu does not use. Contractions with *you* are the ones that matter.

---

## 4 Capitalisation

**ENG-10 Sentence case** for titles, headings, menu items, labels and buttons. Capitalise proper nouns within them.
- ✅ *Faster processes and lower costs with digital credentials* · 🚫 *Faster Processes and Lower Costs With Digital Credentials*

**ENG-11 Do not capitalise a common noun to make it look official:** *the register*, not *the Register*, unless the full formal name is used.

---

## 5 Numbers and dates

**ENG-12 Numbers one to nine in words, 10 upwards in figures.** With units and in tables, always figures: *5 km*, *3%*.

**ENG-13 Dates: day, month, year; no ordinal suffix, no comma.** *23 January 2026*, not *January 23rd, 2026*.

**ENG-14 Times: 24-hour clock with a colon:** *14:30*. German uses a full stop.

**ENG-15 Thousands take a comma, decimals a point, `%` closes up to its figure:** *55,000*, *12.7%*. All three are the reverse of German and French practice; easy to carry over wrongly when drafting in sequence.

---

## 6 Tone

**ENG-16 Second person throughout.** *You* and *your*, never *the user* in copy addressed to that user.

**ENG-17 Plain verbs over nominalisations:** *decide* not *make a decision*, *check* not *carry out a check*.

**ENG-18 Terminology follows `SKILL.md` §9,** including the split between *check* and *verify* (WRD-07).

---

## 7 Gender

How GEN-01 is realised in English. English needs the least of the four.
- Gender-neutral by default: *businessperson*, *chair*, *workforce*, *staffing*.
- Singular *they* where gender is unknown or irrelevant; never *he/she* or *(s)he*.
- Address the reader as *you* wherever the third person would force a choice.
- English has no doublet convention and does not need one. Do not import the German or French pattern.
