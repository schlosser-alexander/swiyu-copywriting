# English

Open this file when writing or correcting English. No federal writing rules cover English, so this file is the whole authority.
- Where it is silent, follow ordinary British editorial practice and add the decision here rather than deciding twice.
- Quotation marks, abbreviations, exclamation marks, dashes, contractions, the lowercase *swiyu*, the second person, plain verbs and gender are settled in `SKILL.md` (MEC-05, MEC-06, MEC-08, MEC-09, MEC-13, NAM-01, AUD-02, WRD-01, GEN-05) and not repeated here.

---

## 1 Spelling and terms

**ENG-01 British English:** *-ise* endings and *-our* spellings. *organise*, *recognise*, *behaviour*, *favour*.
- Oxford *-ize* is also British but reads as American to most people; swiyu does not use it.

**ENG-02 Keep Swiss terms in Swiss form:** *Confederation*, *canton*, *commune*, *Federal Chancellery*. Do not translate an institution's name into a foreign equivalent that suggests a different body.

---

## 2 Punctuation and contractions

**ENG-03 No serial comma** before the final *and* in a list, unless leaving it out creates a real ambiguity.

**ENG-04 Do not contract a noun with *is*, *has*, *does* or *was*.** *The register's open* reads as a possessive on first pass.

---

## 3 Capitalisation

**ENG-05 Sentence case** for titles, headings, menu items, labels and buttons. Capitalise proper nouns within them.
- ✅ *Faster processes and lower costs with digital credentials* · 🚫 *Faster Processes and Lower Costs With Digital Credentials*
- Do not capitalise a common noun to make it look official: *the register*, not *the Register*, unless the full formal name is used.

---

## 4 Numbers and dates

**ENG-06 English number conventions are the reverse of the German and French ones.** Easy to carry over wrongly when drafting the languages in sequence.
- Numbers one to nine in words, 10 upwards in figures. With units and in tables, always figures: *5 km*, *3%*.
- Dates: day, month, year; no ordinal suffix, no comma: *23 January 2026*.
- Times: 24-hour clock with a colon: *14:30*.
- Thousands take a comma, decimals a point, `%` closes up to its figure: *55,000*, *12.7%*.
