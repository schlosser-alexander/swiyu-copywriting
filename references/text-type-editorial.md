# Text type: editorial

Open this file for news items, newsletters, blog posts and presentations – texts the reader chooses to read through rather than scan for an answer.
- Read with the audience rules in `SKILL.md` §3. Editorial texts can address any audience.

---

## 1 News items

**EDT-01 The first sentence carries the news:** what happened, who it affects. Background follows, never precedes.
- ✅ *Ab sofort können Organisationen ihre digitale Kennung im Basisregister eintragen.* · 🚫 *Im Rahmen der laufenden Weiterentwicklung der Vertrauensinfrastruktur wurde ein weiterer Schritt erreicht.*

**EDT-02 Headlines state the finding, not the topic.**
- ✅ *Basisregister ist offen für Eintragungen* · 🚫 *Neues zum Basisregister*

**EDT-03 Date every item, visibly.** An undated news item is unusable a month later.

**EDT-04 Say what the reader should do,** or say explicitly that nothing is required. Ambiguity here generates support requests.

**EDT-05 Do not announce intentions as events.** A decision to plan something is not a milestone (OPS-04).

---

## 2 Quotes and attribution

**EDT-06 Quote real people, named, with their function.** No composite quotes, no invented enthusiasm.

**EDT-07 A quote adds a perspective the surrounding prose cannot give.** A quote that restates the paragraph above it is cut, not shortened.

**EDT-08 Describe partner organisations by role and function, without praise** (VOI-06). Naming a private company as a satisfied customer is not available to swiyu, however good the story.

**EDT-09 SND-01 applies to the sender, not to the speaker.** A quoted person may say *we*; the text around them may not.

---

## 3 Newsletters

**EDT-10 The subject line is the most important sentence.** One thing that happened, under 50 characters, not a teaser.
- ✅ *Basisregister offen für Eintragungen* · 🚫 *Das hat sich bei swiyu getan*

**EDT-11 One lead item, then at most three secondary items.** A newsletter with six equal items has no lead.

**EDT-12 Each item links to one destination,** with link text that names it (BTN-02).

**EDT-13 No greeting that pretends to a relationship.** *Guten Tag* is enough; the salutation rules in the language file apply.

---

## 4 Blog posts

**EDT-14 One argument per post.** The title states it; the closing paragraph does not restate it.

**EDT-15 Open with the situation, not with swiyu.** The reader's problem is the entry point; the infrastructure is the answer, and answers come second (VOI-01).

**EDT-16 Longer form does not licence longer sentences** (MEC-01). More paragraphs, not denser ones.

**EDT-17 Name the limits** (VOI-05). A post with only advantages reads as promotional and costs more credibility than the caveat would have.

---

## 5 Presentations

**EDT-18 One idea per slide,** stated in the slide title as a full statement, not a label.
- ✅ *Digitale Nachweise senken den Prüfaufwand* · 🚫 *Vorteile*

**EDT-19 Bullets are fragments, parallel in construction, six at most** (MEC-03). A slide is not a document.

**EDT-20 Nothing on the slide depends on the speaker.** Decks circulate without their presenter; a slide that only makes sense narrated becomes misinformation.

**EDT-21 Every figure on a slide has a source in the notes** (OPS-04). Numbers travel further than any other content and are quoted without context.

**EDT-22 The closing slide states what happens next,** not *Vielen Dank*.
