# French

Open this file when writing or correcting French. The rules follow the Federal Chancellery's *Instructions sur la présentation des textes officiels en français*.
- Quotation marks and abbreviations are settled in `SKILL.md` (MEC-05, MEC-06) and not repeated here.
- The deviation from the federal instructions on *p. ex.* and *etc.* is recorded in `SOURCES.md`.

---

## 1 Typography and spacing

**FRA-01 Thin non-breaking spaces before all double punctuation,** including the colon. This is Swiss practice; French practice uses a full non-breaking space before the colon.

**FRA-02 Non-breaking spaces bind figures to what follows or precedes them:** *10 heures*, *50 francs*, *3 %*, *10 kg*, *art. 7*, *p. ex.*, *12 h 30*, *29 fr. 50*.

**FRA-03 Square brackets only for a parenthesis inside a parenthesis.** Otherwise round brackets.

**FRA-04 Accented capitals keep their accents,** and *ç* keeps its cedilla: *le Conseil des États*. Acronyms are exempt: *CE*, *CEE*.

## 2 Numbers

**FRA-05 Figures with units, times, currencies, dates, ages and sanctions:** *10 km*, *14 heures*, *10 francs*, *le 23 janvier 2026*, *les assurés de moins de 16 ans*.

**FRA-06 Elsewhere, decide by context and stay consistent.**
- In a text that is not about figures, write numbers out; in one that is, use figures.
- Within a sentence, all numbers take the same form.
- Avoid opening a sentence with a figure. Avoid writing complex numbers out.

**FRA-07 Above four digits, group with a non-breaking space every three digits:** *55 000*, *12 300 000*.
- Four-digit numbers are not grouped: *1700*, *5400*.
- **The apostrophe is not allowed in numbers.**

**FRA-08 Decimal comma:** *4,16 millions de francs*.

**FRA-09 Ordinals:** *1er*, feminine *1re*; then *2e*, *3e*, *4e*, unchanged in the feminine.

---

## 3 Abbreviations

**FRA-10 Introduce an abbreviation in brackets at first use,** then use it consistently.

**FRA-11 Time and currency units are not abbreviated in running text.**
- *heure* shortens to *h* only between figures: *5 h 30*.
- *franc* shortens to *fr.* only between figures: *23 fr. 50*, never *fr. 50.–*.

**FRA-12 Currency codes follow ISO 4217 after a figure** in financial contexts: *300 CHF*, *10 000 USD*.

**FRA-13 *mio.* and *mia.* are not allowed.** Write *million* and *milliard* out.

---

## 4 Capitalisation

**FRA-14 Bodies and authorities take a capital only in their full official name:** *le Conseil fédéral*, *le Département fédéral de l'intérieur*; but *le département concerné*, *le gouvernement*.

**FRA-15 Titles of people take no capital:** *le président de la Confédération*. Capitals only where courtesy demands them, as in salutations.

**FRA-16 Titles of official texts take no capital,** except the Constitution and international agreements cited in full: *le code civil*, *la loi du 4 octobre 1991 sur les forêts*; but *la Constitution*.

## 5 Gender

How GEN-01 is realised in French. French has no participle solution; the moves are epicene nouns, collective nouns, the function instead of the person, relative constructions, and the full doublet.

**FRA-17 Work down the ladder in its French form.** Stop at the first step that works. The step numbers are those of GEN-01.

1. **Take the person out of the sentence** – name the action, the function or the organisation. *Fitness la Frite souhaite vérifier …* · *Signaler vérificateur*
2. **A term that has one form for everyone** (epicene or collective). *les personnes, la clientèle, le personnel, l'électorat*
3. **Participle used as a noun.** No French equivalent; skip this step, do not attempt one.
4. **Describe instead of labelling.** *les personnes utilisant …* · *l'entité qui délivre* · *l'entité qui vérifie*
5. **Full doublet, feminine first** – running text only, and only where visibility is the point. *les utilisatrices et les utilisateurs* · *à toutes celles et ceux*
6. **Plain masculine agreement** where steps 1 to 5 fail. Reached for adjectives and past participles only (FRA-20), never for a role noun.

Short labels use steps 1, 2 and 6. Running text can reach 4 and 5.

**FRA-18 Forbidden outright** by the federal French guide: abbreviated doublets in any form (`·`, `⸱`, `-e-`, `(e)`, `/`), typographic gender markers, and neologisms.

**FRA-19 Ecosystem roles follow the glossary** (GEN-04): *émetteur*, *vérificateur*, *titulaire*.

| Role | Label | Running text |
|---|---|---|
| Issuer | *Émis par* | *l'émetteur* |
| Verifier | *Vérifié par* | *le vérificateur* |
| Holder / user | *Titulaire*; otherwise name the action (step 1) | *les titulaires*; singular *la personne titulaire*; *vous* where the reader is meant |

- *Émetteur* and *vérificateur* name an organisation, not a person, so the ladder does not apply to them. They are masculine because the nouns are; do not "balance" them with a feminine variant.
- *Titulaire* is epicene and resolves at step 2.

**FRA-20 Adjectives and past participles agree in the plain masculine** where no neutral construction is available. Splitting the agreement is forbidden.

| 🚫 | ✅ |
|---|---|
| Comment préférez-vous être contacté(e) ? | Comment préférez-vous être contacté ? |
| Comment préférez-vous être contactée et contacté ? | *(same)* |
| Vérifiée / Vérifié(e) | Vérifié |
| Les vérificatrices et vérificateurs contacté(e)s | Les personnes contactées |

- Prefer the last row's route: an epicene noun resolves role and agreement together, and *personnes* being feminine makes *contactées* correct rather than a compromise.

**FRA-21 Salutations.** *Bonjour,* · *Bonjour [Name],* · *Bonjour à toutes et à tous* · *Chères utilisatrices, chers utilisateurs,* where the doublet is warranted.
- 🚫 *Chère Madame, cher Monsieur,* · 🚫 *Chers/chères utilisateurs/trices*
